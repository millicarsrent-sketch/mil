Tourist Tips for Baku

Insider advice to help you get the very most out of your Baku visit

Baku is a city of fascinating contrasts — medieval stone lanes inside the Old City walls sit alongside flame-shaped glass skyscrapers on the Caspian waterfront. Here are the insider tips that will help you get the most out of your visit.

Getting Around

Baku has a clean and affordable metro (24 lines), but a rental car gives you unbeatable flexibility, especially for reaching the outskirts and making day trips to Gobustan (60 km), Absheron (30 km) or even Shamakhi (120 km). Browse our fleet from 25 AZN/day.

Must-See Attractions

  • Icheri Sheher (Old City) — UNESCO World Heritage Site with the Maiden Tower and Palace of the Shirvanshahs
  • Flame Towers — the iconic trio of towers visible from everywhere in the city
  • Heydar Aliyev Center — Zaha Hadid's masterpiece, a must for architecture fans
  • Boulevard (Bulvar) — the 3.5 km Caspian seafront promenade
  • Gobustan National Park — prehistoric rock art and mud volcanoes, 60 km south (day trip by car)

Best Time to Visit

Spring (April–June) and autumn (September–October) offer the most comfortable temperatures (18–26 °C). Summer can be very hot (up to 40 °C) but the city stays lively. Winter is mild and rarely sees snow.

Money and Payments

The local currency is the Azerbaijani Manat (AZN). Card payments are widely accepted in hotels and restaurants, but carry cash for markets, petrol stations and roadside eateries. ATMs are plentiful in the city centre.

Language and Communication

Azerbaijani is the official language; Russian is widely spoken, especially among older residents and in service industries. English is common in hotels, restaurants and tourist areas. Download a translation app as a backup.

Day Trips Worth Making

With a car, the entire country opens up. Top day trips: Gobustan, Gabala and Shamakhi. Overnight options: Sheki (310 km), Lahij (180 km). Read our driving rules guide before you head out of the city.
